\chapter{Choosing the Grid in Section \ref{DWRMatt}}\label{AppendMax}

In this appendix, we explore the effect of restricting $\operatorname{Grid} \subset \mathbb{C}$ to complex numbers $z$ satisfying $\operatorname{Re}(z) \ge 2.$ We consider this restriction when applying our adaptive mesh refinement technique of \Cref{AMR} to the auxiliary eigenproblems that arise when applying \Cref{pseudospec} to the Maxwell operator \eqref{max op}. 

% In this appendix, we investigate the effect of restricting Grid⊂C to points z satisfying ℜ(z)≥2. We consider this restriction when applying our adaptive mesh refinement technique to the auxiliary eigenproblems arising in Colbrook's algorithm, Algorithm \operatorname{Re}f{pseudospec}, for the Maxwell operator \eqref{max op}.

Let $A$ denote the self-adjoint Maxwell operator given by \eqref{max op}. Furthermore, let $F(z) \defeq (A-zI)^*(A-zI) = (A-\overline{z}I)(A-zI)$ denote the folded operator for Colbrook's algorithm. Consider $u \in N(A),$ where $N(A)$ denotes the nullspace of $A.$ Applying $F(z)$ to $u$ yields 
\begin{align}
    F(z) u &= (A-\overline{z}I)(A-zI) u = (A-\overline{z}I)(-zu) = |z|^2 u.
\end{align}
Since $N(A)$ is infinite-dimensional, $|z|^2$ is an eigenvalue of the folded operator with infinite multiplicity. 

More generally, if $\lambda_j$ is a positive eigenvalue of $A$ with corresponding eigenfunction $u_j,$ then,
\begin{align}
    F(z) u_j &= (A-\overline{z}I)(A-zI) u_j = |\lambda_j - z|^2 u_j.
\end{align}

For Colbrook's algorithm, we want to compute the bottom of the spectrum of $F(z).$ To avoid the eigenvalue $|z|^2$ of infinite multiplicity, we want to ensure that there is at least one eigenvalue of the folded operator that is smaller than $|z|^2.$ To do so, we want to ensure that there is a positive eigenvalue $\lambda_j$ of $A$ that satisfies
\begin{equation}
    | \lambda_j - z |^2 < |z|^2.
\end{equation}
Setting $z = x+iy,$ we can write this last inequality as 
\begin{equation}
    | (\lambda_j-x) - iy |^2 < x^2 + y^2.
\end{equation}
The left-hand side can be expanded, and the $y^2$ can be cancelled, to give
\begin{equation}
    (\lambda_j - x)^2 < x^2, 
\end{equation}
or
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_j^2 - 2 \lambda_j x   < 0.
\end{equation}
Since $\lambda_j > 0,$ the desired condition is
\begin{equation}
    x > \lambda_j / 2.
\end{equation}
Thus, if there exists a positive eigenvalue $\lambda_j$ of $A$ such that $\operatorname{Re}(z) > \lambda_j / 2,$ then $|z|^2$ will not be the smallest eigenvalue of the folded operator. The smallest nonzero eigenvalue of $A$ is $\lambda = 1.$ So, as long as we can guarantee that for every test point $z,$ $\operatorname{Re}(z) > 1/2,$ then $|z|^2$ will not be at the bottom of the spectrum of $F(z).$ Following this line of thought, for the numerical experiments of this report, we considered test points $z$ satisfying $\operatorname{Re}(z) \ge 2.$ In this way, we avoid computing the infinite-multiplicity eigenvalue $|z|^2$ at the bottom of the spectrum.
