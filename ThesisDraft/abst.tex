In recent work, Matthew Colbrook has developed a new approach for computing the spectra and pseudospectra of infinite-dimensional operators. Under certain conditions on the operator, this approach yields convergent approximations that do not suffer from the common pitfalls of the standard discretise-then-solve approach.

In this dissertation, we develop a variational formulation and finite element realisation of Colbrook's approach for computing the pseudospectra of infinite-dimensional operators. The resulting algorithm iterates over test points in the complex plane, solves two generalised eigenproblems at each test point, and uses the smallest computed eigenvalues to approximate the pseudospectra of the underlying operator. Since this algorithm requires repeated eigensolves over potentially many test points, its implementation can be computationally expensive.

In an effort to improve both the accuracy and efficiency of this algorithm, we employ two levels of adaptivity. Our inner adaptivity uses the dual-weighted residual method to drive an adaptive mesh refinement technique for the auxiliary eigenproblems at each test point. Our outer adaptivity aims to reduce the number of eigensolves required for accurate pseudospectral computations by adaptively selecting test points in the complex plane. Our main algorithm uses these two levels of adaptivity to focus the computational effort of Colbrook's approach near a pseudospectral contour of interest. We demonstrate the success of this coupling by applying it to a one-dimensional Dirac operator, to the two-dimensional Maxwell operator, and to the one-dimensional advection--diffusion operator.
